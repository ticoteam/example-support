# Write a reply

Used by every triage pass. A draft is a proposal a person sends. You never send it.

## Shape of a draft
1. **What they asked**, in one line, at the top of the draft on the task (not part of the message):
   `Asks: how to change delivery day; mentions second failed attempt.`
2. **The message**: greet by the name they signed with, answer the question in the first two
   sentences, then the steps (numbered, at most five), then what happens next and when, only if a
   source says so.
3. **Gaps**: every place a decision is needed is `[NEEDS DECISION: ...]`, and the task says who decides.
4. **Sources**: the entry in `knowledge/answers.md` or the doc the answer came from.

## Rules
- Answer from a source you can point at. If there is none, the draft says what you do know and asks
  the owner for the rest. Do not assemble a policy.
- No promises: no refund, credit, discount, fix, or date unless a person has already decided it on the task.
- Match their language and tone; never louder than they are. An upset customer gets one sentence that
  acknowledges the specific problem, not a paragraph of apology.
- Never quote their personal details back beyond what they need to recognize the reply.
- Never blame the customer, and never say "as I said" about something a person said.
- Under 150 words unless the steps need more.

## Check before you hand it over
Read it as the customer: do they know what to do next? Read it as the reviewer: can they send it after
one edit, and do they know which claim came from where? If a check fails twice after rewriting, keep
it as a draft with a note on what is missing instead of polishing.
